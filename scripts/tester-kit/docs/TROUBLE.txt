TROUBLE FILES: what each one is for
===================================

These files are awkward on purpose: real downloads and rips are often like
this. ReCut should handle every one of them without crashing, freezing or
damaging your project, and say clearly what is wrong. If it does not, please
tell us (see REPORTING.md).


Truncated download.mp4
  What:  a 30-second video cut off after less than half of its data, like a
         download that stopped early.
  Good:  it imports. Playback and export stop or go black where the data ends,
         with no crash. A warning about the damaged file is a bonus.

Broken file (no index).mp4
  What:  an MP4 whose index is missing. No program can read it.
  Good:  import refuses it with a clear message that names the file, and the
         rest of the import goes on.

Video only (no audio).mp4
  What:  picture with no sound track at all.
  Good:  it imports and plays; only a video clip goes on the timeline (no empty
         audio clip); export works and the sound is silence.

Audio only (no video).mp4
  What:  an .mp4 that holds sound but no picture.
  Good:  ReCut treats it as audio (it goes into the Audio bin and onto an audio
         track), with a waveform.

One frame.mp4
  What:  a video exactly one frame (1/24 s) long.
  Good:  it imports, shows its picture, and can be put on the timeline and
         stretched like a still. Nothing divides by zero.

Café Ünïcödé – fan édit 🎬.mp4
  What:  a normal clip with spaces, accents, a long dash and an emoji in its
         name.
  Good:  the name shows correctly everywhere; import, play, save, reopen,
         Collect Project and export all work.

Two hours (long timeline).mp4
  What:  two hours of a running timecode on a plain background, with a short
         beep at every minute.
  Good:  a two-hour timeline stays responsive: zooming, scrolling, cutting,
         undo and saving are quick. The timecode shown in the picture matches
         ReCut's timecode.

Messy subtitles.srt (with Messy subtitles.mp4)
  What:  a subtitle file with every common mistake: blocks out of order,
         lines that overlap, a block with no number, dots instead of commas in
         the times, a line that ends before it starts, an empty line, and a
         line that runs past the end of the video.
  Good:  importing Messy subtitles.mp4 picks the file up. ReCut keeps 9 lines,
         sorted by time, and warns about the 2 it could not use properly (the
         empty one and the one that ends before it starts). Nothing crashes.
